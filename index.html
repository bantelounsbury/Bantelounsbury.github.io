About

A path built from the ground up — literally at the bedside.

I started in healthcare the way most administrators never do: at the bedside. As a nurse aide on med-surg, PCU, and inpatient rehab units, I saw firsthand where hospital systems support patients well and where they don't.

That experience sent me looking for leverage. A health informatics degree taught me to see care as data and process. A year inside a top-tier bank's analyst program taught me to see it as a financial system that has to balance. A research role applying generative AI to patient education taught me what's coming next.

I'm now pursuing my MHA at Columbia to bring those threads together. Training for the kind of healthcare leadership that understands the floor, the ledger, and the roadmap all at once.

Featured work

Two projects that shaped how I think about healthcare and data.

Applied AI research

RAG-based patient education for spinal cord injury

Challenge
Patients with newly acquired spinal cord injury need trustworthy, tailored self-management education, but general-purpose LLMs risk surfacing broad, unvetted information.
Approach
Co-developed a retrieval-augmented generation system combining curated clinical resources with GPT-4o and Llama 3.3-70B, then evaluated output quality using NDCG, BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, and BERT F1.
Outcome
Curated content significantly outperformed broader knowledge bases (p<.05). Findings presented at the Pitt AI in Healthcare Research Symposium.
Machine learning

Maternal mortality risk prediction

Challenge
Maternal mortality in underdeveloped communities is often preventable with earlier clinical intervention, but risk is hard to flag in time with limited resources.
Approach
Built a Python model using logistic regression and random forest algorithms trained on health indicators such as age and blood pressure.
Outcome
Achieved 95% prediction accuracy, demonstrating how lightweight ML models could support earlier intervention in low-resource settings.
